03 / Historical chronology

Seven decades of history

The chronology below provides the broad historical framework. Individual dates and biographies can be expanded as the Greyson canon is finalized.

1970s — FOUNDATION

The founding circle

A small circle of associates in Sinaloa begins to use the Greyson name. Early identity is based on personal relationships and shared allegiance.

LATE 1970s — CONSOLIDATION

A recognizable name

The network develops a more consistent identity and a circle of senior figures, while remaining informal and personality-driven.

1980s — TRANSITION

Generational change

Leadership distinctions become clearer. The organization faces the challenge of maintaining cohesion as its network grows.

1990s — REPUTATION

Rivalry and public attention

The Greyson name becomes more prominent in accounts of Sinaloa’s criminal landscape, while competition and internal disagreements intensify.

2000s — PRESSURE

Expansion under scrutiny

External pressure and shifting relationships test the organization’s inherited leadership traditions and public standing.

2010s — FRAGMENTATION

Competing claims

Leadership disputes and generational differences challenge the idea of one uncontested Greyson authority.

MODERN PERIOD — CONTINUITY

The Vierki Greyson era

Vierki Greyson is identified as the organization’s leader in the current Greyson record. The modern period is defined by questions of continuity, identity, and legacy.

04 / Institutional identity

Organization & internal culture

Leadership tradition

Greyson leadership has historically relied on recognition, seniority, personal reputation, and relationships among influential members. Succession has remained a recurring question across generations.

Internal identity

Loyalty, discretion, family history, and shared memory appear repeatedly in accounts of the organization. These themes have also been sources of tension when personal interests diverge.

Historical divisions

Rival claims to authority and regional differences have contributed to periods of fragmentation. The Greyson name has sometimes continued across groups with competing interpretations of its legacy.

Regional context

Sinaloa is central to the organization’s identity. Its communities have distinct histories, and the organization’s reputation and perceived presence have varied across locations and periods.
